Boiler symptoms and what they usually mean.

Common boiler problems.
Symptom.What it often points to.
No heat, no hot water.Failed ignition, tripped safety, no fuel or power, or a dead circulator.
Radiators cold at the top.Trapped air that needs bleeding.
Gauge reading low.Water loss from a leak, or a failed expansion tank.
Banging or kettle-like noise.Scale buildup or low water flow.
Water on the floor.Leaking valve, pump seal, expansion tank or boiler body.
Boiler cycles on and off quickly.Oversized load, a dirty sensor or a flow problem.

Many of these are fixable in one visit. A leak from the boiler body itself, or a heat exchanger that has cracked, usually is not.

Before we arrive: safety first when the heat is out.

A boiler brings fuel, water and electricity together. A few checks are safe and help us.

Smell gas? Leave.

If you smell gas or rotten eggs, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches. Call us after.

Check the basics.

Look at the thermostat, the power switch on or near the boiler, the breaker and the fuel supply. For oil, check the tank gauge.

Read the gauge.

Note the pressure and temperature. Most home boilers sit around the low end of the dial when cold. Write down any error code.

Do not keep resetting.

One reset is fine. Repeated resets can flood the combustion chamber or wear out the ignition.

Older neighborhoods and older boilers.

The Ridgewood Public Library's local-history pages describe neighborhoods that date to different eras, including Brookside around 1890, Floral Park in the 1910s and The Heights between 1892 and 1930. Homes of that age often kept their original heating layout, even when the boiler has been replaced.

That matters for repairs. Older steam and hot water radiator systems have long pipe runs, many valves and parts that can be hard to match. A modern boiler paired with old piping may need balancing or new vents to heat evenly.

We do not assume a house's age from its address. We look at the boiler, the pipes and the distribution before saying what is practical.

Repair or replace the boiler.

Repair makes sense when.

  • The failed part is a pump, ignitor, sensor or valve.
  • The boiler is otherwise clean and quiet.
  • The heat exchanger shows no cracks or heavy leaks.
  • The unit has not needed repeated major repairs.

Talk replacement when.

  • The heat exchanger or boiler body leaks.
  • Repair parts are discontinued.
  • Repairs keep coming back in the same season.
  • Efficiency has dropped and fuel use keeps rising.

According to the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, plumbing work may only be performed by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. The state plumbing subcode adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), the standard we work to on the water side of a boiler. Gas and oil burners have their own requirements, and we tell you plainly which parts of a job we handle and which need a specialist.

If the boiler also feeds your domestic hot water, see water heater repair for the related tank-side work. A leaking boiler can mimic a pipe leak, so a leak detection visit sometimes helps pin down the source. Customers in Glen Rock can find nearby coverage on the Glen Rock page.
