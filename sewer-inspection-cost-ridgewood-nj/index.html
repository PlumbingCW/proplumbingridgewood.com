CCTV Sewer Inspection Cost: A Guide for Ridgewood Homeowners.

A sewer camera inspection is the only way to see what is happening inside a buried line without digging. It is also one of the cheapest steps in any sewer decision, compared with the repairs it can prevent.

Here is what sets the price of an inspection, what you should get for it, and how to use the video before you spend money on a repair.

What a sewer camera inspection actually shows.

A technician feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible rod into your sewer line, usually through a cleanout. The camera sends live video to a monitor, and many systems also track the distance traveled and the position of the camera underground.

You see the pipe material, the joints, the slope, and anything that does not belong. That includes roots, cracks, offset joints, grease, scale and sections where the pipe has collapsed or sagged.

Root intrusion.

Fine roots enter at joints and cracks, then grow into a mass that catches paper and waste. The video shows where and how heavy.

Cracks and breaks.

Clay and cast iron can crack with age and ground movement. A camera finds the exact distance from the cleanout.

Bellies and sags.

A low spot holds water and waste, and it causes repeat clogs even after cleaning. The camera shows standing water along the run.

Offsets and separations.

Joints that have shifted let soil and roots in. These appear as a step or gap between pipe sections.

Why homeowners order an inspection.

Some people call after a backup, and others schedule an inspection as part of a planned decision. Both are good reasons, and the second is often cheaper in the long run.

  • Before buying a home, especially one with mature trees or older piping.
  • After repeated clogs in the main line or a slow drain throughout the house.
  • Before a basement renovation, an addition or a bathroom move that adds load to the line.
  • When a sewer smell appears in the yard or basement.
  • After a cleaning, to confirm that the line is clear and the pipe is sound.
  • Before relining or repair, so the contractor knows exactly what to fix.

The factors that set the price.

What moves the cost of a CCTV sewer inspection.

Prices vary by company, but these are the items that change a quote from one house to the next.

Cleanout accessA clean, reachable cleanout keeps the visit short. Without one, the plumber may need to pull a toilet to get in.
Line lengthA long run from the house to the street takes more time to scan.
Condition of the pipeA heavily blocked line may need cleaning before the camera can pass.
LocatingMarking the position of a defect on the surface requires a locator and more time.
Report and videoA written report with video files and notes costs more than a quick verbal review.
Property typeA multi-unit building or commercial property may have more lines to scan.
TimingSame-day or after-hours service often costs more than a scheduled visit.

Ask whether cleaning, locating and the written report are included, since those are the usual add-ons.

What Ridgewood's code says about house sewers.

Ridgewood's Village Code, Chapter 238, sets material rules for house sewers. It lists cast iron or vitrified clay for sewers beyond three feet from the building, and says sewers must be laid deep enough to be protected from frost.

That matters when you read your video. Cast iron scales and clay cracks and takes in roots at the joints, and those are exactly the conditions a camera picks up in older lines.

Reading the results.

Ask the technician to explain the video as it plays. A good one points out the pipe material, the joints and each defect, and tells you how far from the cleanout it sits.

Not every defect needs a repair. A single hairline crack with no roots may only need monitoring, while a collapsed section needs work soon. Ask for the plumber's honest ranking, from urgent to watch.

What happens after the inspection.

If the line is sound, you leave with confidence and a baseline recording for the future. If it is not, the video lets you compare repair options based on the same facts.

Spot repairs, pipe lining and full replacement all depend on where and how the pipe has failed. Our sewer line repair page explains those choices, and the camera record is what makes a fair bid possible.

How the equipment works, and why it matters to the quote.

Push cameras are built for house laterals. They use a flexible rod with a sealed lens and lights, and they can bend through cleanout fittings and long runs. A good unit records video and shows footage counters, so each defect has a measured position.

Cheaper or older gear produces fuzzy video and no distance data. That is not a deal breaker for a quick look, but it limits what a repair crew can do with it. When you book, ask about the recording and whether you receive a copy of the file. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what a standard visit covers.

Inspecting before you buy a home.

A general home inspection does not look inside the sewer line. For an older house, or any lot with large trees near the lateral, a camera inspection is a practical addition to the purchase process.

Schedule it during the inspection period so you can still negotiate. A defect found after closing becomes your repair, but one found before can change the price or lead to a seller credit.

Mistakes that make an inspection less useful.

  • Hiring someone who cannot show you the video live or deliver a copy.
  • Skipping cleaning on a line that is partly blocked, so the camera cannot pass.
  • Not asking where the pipe changes material, which is often where trouble starts.
  • Accepting a verbal diagnosis with no distance or location marked.
  • Treating the inspection as the repair, when it is only the first step.
