Tankless Water Heater in Ridgewood, NJ.

A tankless heater heats water as it flows, so there is no tank to fill, store or rust through. The trade is that the unit leans harder on your gas line, your vent and your water quality.

Is tankless the right choice for your household?.

Tankless suits households that run out of hot water with a tank, or that want floor space back. It delivers hot water as long as the burner runs, so back to back showers are not a problem as long as the unit is sized for them.

It is a weaker fit when the gas line is small, the vent cannot be routed cleanly or the water is very hard and nobody will maintain the unit. If you are weighing it against a standard tank, our water heater installation page lays out the full range of unit types.

Why Ridgewood's hard water matters to a tankless unit.

Ridgewood Water's 2019 data report listed hardness from 136 to 470 ppm as calcium carbonate. In grains per gallon, that is roughly 8 to 27 by our arithmetic, which is hard to very hard. The utility serves Ridgewood, Glen Rock, Midland Park and Wyckoff, so homeowners there face the same conditions.

A tankless heat exchanger has narrow passages, and scale builds in them quickly. Without periodic flushes, you can see temperature swings, reduced flow, noisy operation and eventually error codes that stop the unit.

The practical answer is a service schedule and, for many homes, a softener or scale inhibitor ahead of the heater. Check your manufacturer's maintenance guidance, because skipping scale control can affect the warranty.

Tankless and tank heaters, side by side.

Where tankless works well.

  • Continuous hot water for large families.
  • A wall unit that frees floor space.
  • Wide vent options on sidewall models.
  • Longer expected service life when maintained.

Where a tank may be simpler.

  • Small gas line that would need upsizing.
  • Hard water with no plan for scale control.
  • Tight budgets for the initial install.
  • Homes where the old flue can be reused.

Descaling flushes and error codes.

A flush circulates a mild descaling solution through the heat exchanger using the service valves, then rinses the unit clean. It is routine maintenance, and a good time to clean the inlet filter screen.

When a tankless heater shows a code, the cause is often scale, a blocked vent, low gas pressure or a flow sensor problem. We read the code, check the likely cause and fix it. If the unit is leaking or has stopped heating entirely, see our water heater repair page, and if it is beyond saving, water heater replacement.
