Wet basements come first in Glen Rock.

Water behaves badly here. During the remnants of Hurricane Ida on the night of September 1 to 2, 2021, Diamond Brook and the Saddle River overflowed, and flooding hit The Boulevard, Lowell Road, Prospect Street and the county park. Many basements filled, and one foundation was compromised. The borough counted 87 storm calls that night.

The borough posted a new notice on September 24, 2026, saying recent rains caused flooding in areas of town that had never flooded before. Whatever your street's history, a basement that stayed dry for decades is no longer a safe assumption.

A sump pump that fails during a storm is the usual first call. If yours hums but does not move water, or runs without stopping, our sump pump repair work covers the pit, the float and the discharge line. If water keeps coming in through the walls, basement waterproofing deals with the cause instead of the symptom.

Who owns which sewer pipe on your lot.

Glen Rock sewer laterals belong to the homeowner.

Under Glen Rock's sewer ordinance, the borough maintains the public sewer. The owner maintains the house lateral and the building sewer lateral, including the stretch that runs through the right of way or an easement. A new or replaced lateral needs a cleanout cap at the curb, and a written permit from the Board of Health is required before anyone uncovers, connects or alters a building sewer lateral.

The same chapter says roof leaders and foundation drains may not tie into the sanitary building sewer unless approved. Check with the borough before connecting any drain or sump discharge to a sewer line.

Source type: Glen Rock Borough Code, Chapter 171 (Sewers), on ecode360

In practice this means a backup that starts in your lateral is your repair.

The borough's Department of Public Works does clear public sewers and handles root control, and you can reach it at (201) 670-3980, or through Central Dispatch at (201) 652-3800 after hours. If the main in the street is the problem, they will want to hear about it. If it is your line, we start with a sewer camera inspection so you can see the break or the roots before anyone digs.

Old housing, original piping.

About 79 percent of Glen Rock's structures were built before 1970, and 93.3 percent are single-family houses. The borough grew mostly between the 1900s and the 1930s, and its housing runs from early cottages to Mission, Tudor and Colonial Revival houses. A house that old may still carry some of its original supply or drain piping, and the only way to know is to look.

We do not assume a material from a build date. We open a cleanout, run a camera, or check the pipe where it enters the basement, then tell you what is there and whether it is worth replacing.

Slow main drain.

A kitchen sink and a basement floor drain that both gurgle usually point to the main line, not the fixture. Drain cleaning clears it from a cleanout.

Lead and galvanized service lines.

Ridgewood Water supplies the borough and plans to replace all lead and galvanized service lines by 2031. Our water service line repair covers your side of the connection.

Water heater failure.

A rusted tank leaking onto a finished basement floor needs quick action. See water heater replacement.

Ridgewood Water and your service line.

Glen Rock gets its water from Ridgewood Water, which also serves Midland Park, Wyckoff and Ridgewood. According to the utility's 2024 report, about 1,700 customer-side lead service lines were counted across the four towns, and 31 facilities were being consolidated into 12 PFAS treatment plants by the end of 2026. Those are system-wide numbers, not a Glen Rock test of your tap.

The utility runs an opt-in lead line replacement program, and Glen Rock addresses are eligible. Ridgewood Water covers its side, the owner pays for the private side, and there is a look-up tool on its website. If you plan to join, line up the plumbing work before the crew arrives so the two sides meet cleanly.

Before we arrive in Glen Rock.

Basements are where most of our Glen Rock calls start. These steps limit the damage.

Find the main shutoff.

Turn off the water where the line enters the house, usually in the basement near the meter. A stuck valve is worth telling us about.

Cut power to wet areas.

If water is near outlets, a furnace or a panel, do not wade in. Shut off the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Stop using drains.

In a sewage backup, do not flush or run sinks, showers or the washer. Every gallon goes to the lowest opening.

Move what you can.

Lift boxes and stored items off the floor. Take photos of the water line for your insurer before you clean up.
