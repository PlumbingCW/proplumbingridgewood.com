Sewer or septic: the question we ask first.

Not all of Wyckoff is connected to a public sewer. The township's 2010 Master Plan reexamination said significant areas continue to be unsewered, and the Northwest Bergen County Utilities Authority is the sewer contact on the township's utilities page. Sewers have been extended in pieces since then, including a project on Sicomac Avenue and a requirement for sewer lines on Terhune Terrace.

So a gurgling drain in Wyckoff has two possible destinations, and the right fix depends on which one your house uses. Your deed, a past inspection report or a call to the township can answer it.

Wyckoff is only partly sewered.

The township's own planning documents describe large areas without public sewers and consider newer septic technology. A 2020 Planning Board record shows the township moving to require sanitary sewer lines on Terhune Terrace, and a 2021 authority resolution covers a sewer extension at 359 Sicomac Avenue. The percentage of the township that is sewered has not been published in the documents we reviewed.

If you are not sure how your house drains, ask the township before you buy a house, dig, or schedule a lateral replacement.

Source type: Wyckoff Township Master Plan reexamination (2010), Planning Board minutes (2020), NBCUA resolution (2021)

When we are called for a backup, we run a camera through the house line and report what we find. If your line leads to a public sewer, sewer line cleaning and a sewer camera inspection cover most causes. If it leads to a tank, we tell you plainly where our work stops.

Large older houses and long pipe runs.

The township's 2010 reexamination called Wyckoff an older community in terms of housing, with most homes built before 1960 and more than 89 percent single-family detached. Its 2025 housing plan adds that rentals are only 9.1 percent of occupied units and that much of the stock is big enough for multigenerational living. Owner-occupancy is 92.7 percent.

Big houses mean big heating loads and long pipe runs. A no-heat call in January is often a boiler repair, and a cold shower is often a water heater repair before it becomes a replacement.

Permits for boilers and water heaters.

According to Wyckoff's building department, a permit is needed to modernize plumbing, to replace a furnace, boiler or hot water heater, to install or replace a septic system, and to hook up to the town sewer. The forms are F100 plus the subcode forms, with a review time of 20 working days, at 340 Franklin Avenue, (201) 891-7000. Finished work is inspected against the state plumbing subcode, which adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code.

That review window matters on a cold night. If your heater fails in the evening, we can discuss temporary fixes while the paperwork moves, and we tell you which jobs will wait on a permit.

Storms and Route 208.

On September 2, 2021, after Ida, Wyckoff police reported severe flooding in most parts of town, especially on State Route 208. We do not assume your lot is safe because it is far from a stream.

A working sump pump matters in any house with a basement. We handle sump pump repair and installation, and a battery backup is worth discussing if your pump has failed before.

Ridgewood Water in Wyckoff.

Wyckoff's utilities page lists Ridgewood Water for water. The utility draws mainly from 52 deep wells and buys water from other suppliers, and the township's Board of Health said in 2022 that two of the four PFAS treatment plants planned for the four towns would be built in Wyckoff. Its 2024 report listed PFOA and PFOS violations system-wide and hardness between 217 and 441 ppm.

We could not find a township page that says which streets use another supplier or a private well. If you have a private well, the Board of Health points to state testing rules. If you are on the utility, water service line repair covers your side of the pipe to the curb.

Franklin Avenue, Godwin Avenue, Wyckoff Avenue, Goffle Road, Greenwood Avenue and Mountain Avenue all fall inside the township's roads. The southwestern section keeps the old name Sicomac. We do not give drive times or arrival windows, since traffic changes them.
