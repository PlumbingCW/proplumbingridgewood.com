Fair Lawn runs its own water system.

Ridgewood Water does not serve Fair Lawn. The borough's Water Department runs the system, with about 105 miles of main, 1,200 hydrants and four storage tanks. It pumps from 16 wells about 400 feet deep in the Brunswick aquifer, which supply roughly 55 percent of the water, and buys the rest from other suppliers, including the Passaic Valley Water Commission.

If your bill jumps, the water department's number is 201-703-4260. Ask where its responsibility ends at your address, then call us for the side that belongs to you. A leak detection visit traces water that disappears between the meter and your fixtures.

A Superfund history sits under the water supply.

Fair Lawn Well Field was placed on the federal National Priorities List in September 1983. Solvents were found in the Westmoreland wells in 1978, and later testing found 1,4-dioxane and PFAS. The Westmoreland Well Field was disconnected from supply in 2016, and two more plants came offline in 2017.

A new treatment system was reported built in spring 2025 and expected to run by the end of 2026. In the meantime the borough blends water from its Cadmus Place plant with two purchased sources. We cannot give you current compliance numbers, so read the borough's latest water quality report.

Sewer laterals and what the ordinance says.

Fair Lawn's Sewer Division runs the borough system, including pump stations, manholes and mains, and wastewater goes to the Passaic Valley Sewerage Commission in Newark. According to the borough sewer code, the owner is responsible for the lateral from the foundation to the curb. The borough handles the stretch from the curb to the main when a curb cleanout cap exists.

The code also keeps cleanouts accessible and bars downspouts, foundation drains and groundwater from the sanitary sewer unless approved. Fats, oil and grease above 100 ppm are prohibited, and restaurants need grease interceptors.

A backup in your own lateral is your repair, and it starts with a camera inspection. Drain cleaning clears a clog, and a sewer line repair follows if the pipe is cracked. Restaurants anywhere in the borough should keep grease interceptors serviced, which is part of our commercial plumbing work.

Radburn and the age of Fair Lawn housing.

Radburn, a National Historic Landmark since 2005, was planned in 1928 as a town for the motor age. Houses face interior parks, garages sit on cul-de-sacs, and the district mixes Tudor Revival and Colonial Revival homes, brick duplexes and row houses. The first houses sold in May 1929, and the original plan ended in 1934.

Outside Radburn, we do not claim a typical age for Fair Lawn's housing. Owner-occupancy is 78.1 percent across about 12,229 households. We look at the pipes in front of us instead of guessing from a date.

Rivers on two sides.

The Passaic River forms the western edge of Fair Lawn and the Saddle River forms the northeast, east and southeast edges. During Ida in September 2021 and Irene in August 2011, those rivers flooded nearby towns. We have no Fair Lawn-specific flood record, so we do not say your street flooded.

If you live near either river and your basement has taken water before, keep your pump in working order. See our sump pump repair page, and call us if the pit is full and silent.
