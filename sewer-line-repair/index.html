What goes wrong with a house sewer.

A sewer lateral is out of sight, so failures tend to announce themselves late. Knowing the common failure types helps you describe the problem and understand the repair.

Root intrusion.

Fine roots enter at joints seeking moisture, then thicken into a mat that catches paper and waste. Cabling clears them and they return.

Cracked or broken pipe.

Clay and cast iron can crack from age, settling, heavy loads or ground movement. Soil then washes into the pipe.

Belly or sag.

A low spot holds water and solids. The line drains slowly and clogs in the same place over and over.

Collapse or offset joint.

A section drops or shifts. Cables stop at the obstruction and the line backs up completely.

Signs the problem is in the lateral.

  • Several drains back up at once, especially the lowest one in the house.
  • Repeated clogs in the same line a few weeks after cleaning.
  • Gurgling toilets and sewer gas smells near floor drains.
  • Wet or sunken patches in the yard along the path of the line.
  • Cable machines stopping at the same distance each time.

If the same line has clogged more than once, skip another round of drain cleaning and get the pipe looked at. A short video often shows a problem a cable never will.

Emergency steps before we arrive.

A blocked lateral sends wastewater to the lowest open drain. These steps reduce the cleanup.

Stop all water use.

No flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher. Every drop adds to the backup.

Leave the cleanout alone.

Do not open a cleanout cap while sewage is rising behind it. Show us where it is when we arrive.

Keep people clear.

Keep children and pets away from wet areas. Wear rubber gloves and boots if you must move things, and wash up afterward.

Move valuables.

Lift furniture, boxes and electronics off the floor. Turn off the breaker for any outlet in standing water only if you can reach it dry.

Choosing the repair method.

The camera shows what the pipe needs. The right fix depends on how much of the line is damaged, where it sits and what is above it.

Sewer repair options.
Method.When it fits.Trade-off.
Spot repair by excavation.One broken section, collapse or offset joint.Needs a trench, restores that section only.
Pipe lining.Cracks, root-prone joints, long runs under hard surfaces.Needs a pipe that is not collapsed, sizes shrink slightly.
Full replacement.Extensive damage, bellies or failed old material.Largest scope, new pipe over the full run.
Cleaning and monitoring.Light root growth in sound pipe.Buys time, does not repair.

Before lining or digging, the pipe should be cleared and scanned so the repair is based on what is really there. If the line is only grease or scale coated, hydro jetting may clear it without repair.

What a Ridgewood lateral is made of and why it matters.

Ridgewood's village code calls for house sewers to be cast iron or vitrified clay beyond three feet from the building, and to be set deep enough to be protected from frost. New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), so a repaired lateral is built to that standard as well. Both materials have lasted a long time, but clay joints and old iron can crack, rust through or let in roots.

The Library's neighborhood history dates parts of Ridgewood to the 1890s and the 1910s, and others to the 1930s and 1950. Where a lateral has served a house for generations, we expect to see old joints, and a camera tells us whether they are still tight.

Stormwater is another source of trouble. Village code bars sump pump and groundwater discharge from being tied into the sanitary sewer, and violations carry fines and a monthly surcharge. If your pump is piped to the sewer, expect a backup in heavy rain, and plan to correct it as part of the repair. The village also runs its own wastewater treatment plant, so any permit or inspection questions go through the village.
