What to have ready when you call.

A minute of preparation helps the dispatcher understand the job and gives your plumber a head start.

The address.

Give the street, town and any apartment or unit number. If the entrance is hard to find, say so.

What you are seeing.

Describe where the water is, where it is coming from, and whether it is still running. Photos on your phone are useful to show the plumber later.

What you already did.

Tell us whether you closed a valve, flipped a breaker or tried a plunger. That saves time and prevents repeat steps.

Who we should ask for.

Give a name and the best number to reach you. If a tenant or neighbor will meet the plumber, share their name too.

Which problem fits which page.

If you are not sure what to call it, just describe what you see. Dispatchers translate plain descriptions into the right kind of visit every day. A few common matches may help while you dial.

A sink that fills and will not empty belongs under drain cleaning. A water bill that has jumped for no reason belongs under leak detection. A tank that no longer heats is covered under water heater repair, and sewage in a floor drain is a job for sewer line cleaning.

Where we work.

We serve Ridgewood, Glen Rock, Midland Park, Wyckoff, Waldwick and Fair Lawn. If you are in a Bergen County town not listed here, call anyway and we will tell you plainly whether we can come.

Our service areas page gives more detail on each town, and the services page lists every kind of job we take.

What happens after you call.

The dispatcher records the details and gives you any safety steps right away. A plumber is then assigned, and we tell you the best timing we can. We do not promise an exact arrival, because we would rather be honest than wrong.

On site, the plumber inspects the problem and gives you the price before work begins. You approve it, and the repair starts. If the plan changes, we stop and talk to you first.

If you are unsure whether something is an emergency, call anyway. Water near electrical panels, sewage in living space, a gas smell or a flooded basement should always be treated as urgent. If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 first.
