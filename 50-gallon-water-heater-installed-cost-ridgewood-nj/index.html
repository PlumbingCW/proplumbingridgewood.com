What Does a 50 Gallon Water Heater Cost Installed in Ridgewood, NJ?.

A 50 gallon tank is the most common size for a family home, and it is the number people search for when the old one fails. The price you are quoted, though, covers a lot more than the tank.

We do not publish a flat number because the real price depends on your house. This guide shows what goes into an installed price so you can compare quotes fairly.

Why an installed price is not the price of the tank.

The sticker on the tank at a store covers the tank. An installed price covers the tank, the labor, the connections, the disposal of the old unit and any parts the code or your house requires. Two quotes that look far apart may include very different things.

Before you compare numbers, compare what is in them. Ask each company to list what is included and what would be extra. A short, clear list is a good sign. A single line item for everything is not.

Gas, electric or hybrid: the first big split.

The fuel is the biggest single factor in both the tank price and the installation work. Each type has different parts, different venting needs and different running costs afterward.

How fuel type changes a 50 gallon install.
Type.What it needs.Installation effect.
Gas, atmospheric vent.Gas line, flue to a chimney or liner, combustion air.Venting condition often drives the work.
Gas, power vent.Gas line, electric outlet, plastic vent through the wall.More parts, simpler routing in many basements.
Electric.Dedicated circuit of the right size.Fewer parts, but the panel must have room.
Hybrid heat pump.Electric supply, drain for condensate, space for airflow.Higher unit price, more clearance needed.

A hybrid unit can use much less electricity than a standard electric tank, but it needs a larger space and a place for condensate to drain. If you are choosing for efficiency, ask the installer how the numbers work for your use.

Items that appear on almost every quote.

Whatever the fuel, most installed quotes share a short list of items. Check that each one is in the quotes you collect.

🔧

The tank.

The model and its efficiency rating shape the unit price.

🔩

Connections.

New flexible connectors, shutoff valves and fittings so the joints are sound.

🧯

Safety parts.

A temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe.

🚚

Haul away.

Removal and disposal of the old tank.

Those are the basics. A good quote lists them. The next sections cover items that vary from house to house and explain why one installed price can be much higher than another.

Venting and gas work can change the whole job.

A gas tank has to vent combustion gases safely. In many older houses the flue connects to a masonry chimney that was sized for a different appliance. If the chimney is oversized or in poor shape, a liner may be needed or a power vent unit may be the better path.

The gas line must also be the right size and in good condition. A short stretch of pipe, a new shutoff valve or a drip leg may be needed. A plumber who inspects the vent and gas line before quoting will give you a more accurate price.

Where the tank sits affects labor.

A tank in an open basement is easier than one in a closet, an attic or a crawl space. Tight stairs, a finished ceiling or a long carry add time. So does moving a tank that weighs hundreds of pounds once it is filled.

If the heater has to move to a new spot, you add piping, venting and sometimes electrical work. Keeping the location the same keeps the job simpler.

Code items that often add to the price.

Code and safety requirements vary by house. Some items below will apply to you and some will not.

  • An expansion tank, where a closed system or a pressure reducing valve makes one necessary..
  • A drain pan and pipe where a leak could damage a finished floor below..
  • Proper support, clearances and a safe discharge point for the relief valve..
  • New gas connector, shutoff or sediment trap on a gas supply..
  • Dielectric unions or similar fittings where dissimilar metals meet..

Not every house needs each item. Ask the installer which items are required in your case and why. A good answer refers to the setup, not to a standard package.

Ridgewood hard water and your tank.

Ridgewood Water published its water quality report in 2020 with 2019 data. It lists hardness in Ridgewood water between 136 and 470 parts per million as calcium carbonate, and between 97 and 206 in the portion purchased from Suez. On the usual hardness scale, that runs from moderately hard to very hard depending on the source.

Hard water leaves scale on the bottom of a tank and on heating elements. That shortens life and lowers efficiency. If you want the current hardness for your street, check the latest water quality report or call Ridgewood Water at 201-670-5520. Ask the installer about flushing the tank each year.

Running costs after the install.

The installed price is a one-time number. The tank then runs for years, and its running cost can outweigh a small difference in purchase price. Efficiency ratings, insulation and fuel prices all play a part.

Ask how long the warranty on the tank and on the parts lasts, and what it covers. Some warranties cover only the tank shell, while labor is separate. A longer warranty with a better anode rod usually costs more at purchase but can last longer in hard water.

Repair, replace or go tankless.

If your tank is more than a decade old and leaks at the base, replacement is the answer. A tank that is newer and fails because of a thermostat or element may be worth a repair. Our water heater replacement page lays out how that choice works.

Some homeowners also ask about tankless. It changes the whole price picture and needs its own sizing step, which our tankless water heater page covers.
