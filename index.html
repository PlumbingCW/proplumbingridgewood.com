Plumbing help for Ridgewood homes and businesses.

Ridgewood Pro Plumbing is a phone-first plumbing service. There is no booking form and no chat widget. You call, you talk to a real person, and you describe the problem.

We handle the jobs that cannot wait, like a leaking tank, a flooded basement or a main line that has stopped. We also schedule planned work, like a new water heater or a bathroom rough-in. Pick the service below that matches what you have, or just call and we will sort it out together.

Water heater replacement.

A tank that leaks at the base is usually finished. We size the new unit to your household and match the gas or electric supply and the venting.

🌀

Drain cleaning.

Slow kitchen sinks, backed-up tubs and floor drains that gurgle. We clear the line and then run water to confirm it flows.

🛠️

Water heater repair.

No hot water, a pilot that will not stay lit, or a tank that rumbles. Many of these are repairs, not replacements.

💧

Leak detection.

A water bill that keeps climbing or a meter that spins with every fixture off points to a hidden leak. We trace it before anyone cuts a wall.

🚧

Sewer line repair.

Roots, cracks and bellies in the lateral cause repeat backups. We explain your options, from spot repair to full replacement.

Burst pipe repair.

A split supply line can empty gallons into a ceiling in minutes. We stop the water, repair the pipe and tell you what to dry first.

Sump pump repair.

A pump that will not start in a storm turns a basement into a pond. We test, repair or replace it.

♨️

Boiler repair.

Cold radiators or no hot water from a gas or oil boiler get diagnosed on site, with the cause explained in plain words.

Ridgewood details that shape the work we do.

Ridgewood is a village of about 26,500 people, and the Census Bureau puts the owner-occupied rate at 82.5 percent. That is a lot of homeowners managing their own plumbing, from the water service line to the water heater in the basement.

Neighborhoods like Brookside, Floral Park, The Heights and the Old Country Club area each grew in different decades, according to the Ridgewood Public Library local history pages. We treat every house as its own case, because pipe materials and layouts vary from one block to the next.

The water behind your tap.

Ridgewood Water, the village utility, serves Ridgewood, Glen Rock, Midland Park and Wyckoff. According to its published water quality report, most supply comes from groundwater wells, with some purchased water added, and hardness readings in the 2019 data ran from 136 to 470 parts per million.

Hard water leaves scale inside tanks and tankless heaters. If your hot water recovery has slowed down, a water heater repair visit may find buildup, and a tankless water heater flush can clear it.

Source type: Ridgewood Water published water quality report (2019 data)

Lead service lines are another local topic. New Jersey law requires lead and galvanized service lines to be found and removed by 2031, and the utility owns the pipe from the main to the curb while the homeowner owns the pipe from the curb to the house. If you hire a contractor for the private side, the work has to be coordinated with Ridgewood Water at 201-670-5520, and our water service line repair page explains what that job involves.

Sump pumps have a local rule too. The village code bans sending sump or groundwater into the sanitary sewer, so a basement pump should discharge outside. If yours does not, see our sump pump repair page before the next heavy rain.

What affects the price of a plumbing job.

Factors we explain before you approve work.

No two jobs cost the same, so we do not post flat numbers. Here is what moves the price up or down, and you will hear each one on the phone or at your door.

AccessA pipe in an open basement ceiling is quicker to reach than one behind finished drywall or under a slab.
Parts and fixturesA standard faucet cartridge costs less than a specialty valve. Water heaters and boilers vary by size and fuel.
Time of dayNight, weekend and holiday calls take a plumber away from rest and are priced with that in mind.
Scope and permitsSome jobs need a permit and an inspection. Others are a simple repair. We tell you which one yours is.
Condition of the systemOld galvanized piping or corroded shutoffs can add steps to a job that looked simple from the outside.

You always see the price before the work begins. If we find something new once a wall is open, we stop and ask.

Planned work, not only emergencies.

Plenty of calls are not emergencies. Homeowners schedule a water heater installation before the old tank fails, book a sewer camera inspection before buying a house, or ask about repiping when pinhole leaks keep returning.

Planned jobs let you compare options calmly. Call during the day, describe the project, and we will schedule a visit and explain the work in order.

One practical note on water heaters. In New Jersey, replacing a heater with one of like capacity counts as minor work, which means no permit is needed before you start. The enforcing agency still has to be notified before work begins, a permit application is filed within five business days, and an inspection follows. We handle that process with you so nothing is missed.
