Commercial plumbing problems that cannot wait.

Commercial buildings share pipes, run fixtures harder and have more people reporting problems at the same time. A leak in one unit can reach the tenant below within minutes.

The calls we handle most often are below, grouped by who usually calls.

Restaurants.

Backed-up floor drains, grease-clogged kitchen lines, leaking prep sinks, dishwasher drains and hot water loss during service.

Retail shops.

Restroom clogs, running toilets, a leaking water heater in a back room, sink faucet failures and drain odors.

Offices and multi-tenant buildings.

Ceiling leaks, shared riser problems, restroom backups and water heater trouble that affects several tenants.

Salons and service businesses.

Shampoo sink drains clogged with hair, slow floor drains and low hot water pressure for sinks and chairs.

Property managers.

Tenant call-ins, after-hours emergencies and records of what was found and fixed.

If your business has a plumbing emergency, do this first.

A few steps protect stock, floors and the next tenant, and they are all quick.

Stop the water.

Close the fixture stop, or the building main if you cannot find it. Know where the shutoff is before you need it.

Stop using fixtures.

Tell staff not to flush, rinse mop buckets or run dishwashers if the drain is backing up.

Move stock and equipment.

Lift boxes, paper goods and food off the floor, and unplug equipment standing in water.

Tell the landlord or manager.

If you lease, the shutoff or riser may be building property. Call the property manager and tell us who to coordinate with.

Working in Ridgewood's commercial corridors.

Many of Ridgewood's businesses sit along East and West Ridgewood Avenue, including the area around Garber Square, where the NJ Transit Ridgewood Station stands at the foot of West Ridgewood Avenue. Others sit near Route 17 and Franklin Turnpike, both named in the boundary of the Paramus Reformed Church Historic District.

These are not new buildings. The station is on the National Register of Historic Places, as is the Archibald-Vroom House at 160 East Ridgewood Avenue. Older commercial buildings often have mixed piping from several decades of tenants, shared drain lines and basements that were adapted later.

What that means in practice is simple. We look at the whole drain and supply path, not only the fixture that is failing, and we ask the landlord about prior repairs. A restaurant on a shared line may be affected by a neighbor's grease, which is why we trace backups before blaming a unit.

Codes, permits and who signs off.

The Village has a Department of Building and Inspections that enforces the Uniform Construction Code, with a Construction Official and a Plumbing Subcode Official. Plumbing in New Jersey must be performed by a licensed master plumber.

For tenants, that means a repair that changes the piping may need a permit and an inspection. We tell you before starting whether a job triggers one, and we handle the application. According to the state rule on minor work (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.17A), a like-capacity water heater swap needs notice to the village before work starts, an application within 5 business days and an inspection.

If your landlord controls the work, tell us up front. Some leases limit tenants to certain contractors or require notice before any plumbing is opened.

Common commercial jobs and where to read more.

Restaurants and salons that see regular clogs often need hydro jetting to scour grease and buildup from the line, and a stubborn backup in a shared lateral may need sewer line cleaning. Businesses with a failed tank can read about water heater replacement, and a restroom that will not drain is covered in drain cleaning.

Our work is not limited to Ridgewood. Shops in Fair Lawn, a short drive south, can read the Fair Lawn page for coverage.
