Reading what your disposal is telling you.

A disposal usually announces a problem before it quits. The sound it makes, and what the sink does, narrows the cause quickly.

These patterns come up on most calls.

🔇

Hums but does not spin.

The impeller is jammed by a bone, utensil or hard seed. A hex key at the bottom turns the motor by hand to free it.

🔕

Silent and dead.

A tripped reset button on the bottom, a switch or outlet fault, or a burned-out motor.

💧

Water at the bottom of the cabinet.

A failed top seal, a cracked housing or a loose drain connection. Cabinet bases rot fast, so this one is urgent.

🌀

Sink backs up when it runs.

The clog is past the disposal, in the trap or the kitchen branch line.

🔔

Runs but grinds poorly.

Dull shredder ring, a worn motor or too much fibrous waste.

Before we arrive: make the sink safe.

A jammed or leaking disposal needs two things right away, power off and water stopped.

Cut the power.

Switch off the wall switch and unplug the unit under the sink. Never put your hand in the drain, even when it appears off.

Press the reset.

Look for a small red button on the bottom of the disposal. If it popped, press it once after the unit has cooled.

Do not add water or chemicals.

Skip drain cleaner. It sits in the sink and splashes anyone who then works on the pipe.

Shut off the sink.

If the cabinet is wet, close both valves under the sink, and empty the cabinet so the wood can dry.

What the disposal sends down the line in Ridgewood.

The Village of Ridgewood operates its own Water Pollution Control Facility, so what goes down your kitchen drain ends up in a locally run system. Before it gets there, it has to pass through your trap, your branch line and your house sewer.

Grease is the usual culprit. It leaves the disposal as a warm liquid, cools in the pipe and builds a ring that gets thicker with every dish. Coffee grounds, rice and potato peels pack together instead of washing through.

If a kitchen drain keeps slowing after you clear the disposal, the blockage has moved down the line. That is drain cleaning territory, and a main line problem would need sewer line cleaning.

Repair or replace the disposal.

Repair is reasonable when.

  • The unit hums or jams but the motor runs free by hand.
  • A hose, gasket or flange seal is leaking.
  • It is newer and the grind is still good.
  • The reset button restores power.

Replace when.

  • The housing is cracked or rusted through.
  • The motor is seized or burned out.
  • It leaks from the body and not the seals.
  • Repairs would cost near the price of a new unit.

A new disposal also has to match the sink flange and the drain layout. If you are adding a disposal where none exists, an electrician or an outlet under the sink may be needed. We confirm that on the phone so there are no surprises on the day.
