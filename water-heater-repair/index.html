Quick checks that are safe to try.

A few things take two minutes and sometimes solve the problem. Stop if anything feels unsafe, and never remove panels on a unit that is live.

  1. Check the breaker for an electric heater, and reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call..
  2. Look at the thermostat setting. A dial knocked to the lowest mark gives lukewarm water..
  3. On a gas unit, look for a pilot flame through the viewing window. If it is out, follow the lighting instructions on the tank label, once..
  4. Open a hot tap and check whether the flow is steady. Weak flow points at a clogged fixture or valve, not the heater..

If the pilot will not stay lit, or you smell gas at any point, stop. That is a job for a plumber, and the smell is a reason to leave the house first.

Emergency steps if the tank is leaking or smells of gas.

These steps keep a repairable problem from becoming a damaging one.

Shut off the cold supply.

Close the valve on the cold pipe above the tank. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you cannot reach it, close the main water shutoff.

Turn off power or gas.

Switch off the breaker for an electric unit. Set the gas valve on a gas unit to off. Do not relight anything.

Gas smell means leave.

If you smell gas, do not touch switches or phones inside. Go outside, then call the gas utility and ours.

Contain the water.

Place a bucket or tray under a slow drip. For a steady flow, drain the tank to a floor drain with a hose from the drain valve once the power is off.

What Ridgewood water does to a heater.

According to Ridgewood Water's published quality report, hardness ranges from roughly 136 to 470 parts per million across its supply. That is moderately to very hard water, and it leaves scale on elements, thermostats and the tank floor.

Scale is behind a large share of the electric element failures and slow recovery we see. A heater in a hard-water home needs flushing, and its anode rod should be checked before the tank starts to rust through.

The same water also shortens the life of the relief valve and drain valve, which stick open or weep once mineral crust forms on the seat. Replacing a weeping relief valve is cheap. Ignoring one can soak a basement floor.

When repair stops making sense.

A leak through the tank wall cannot be fixed. A rusted-out base, a cracked tank liner or a heavy scale crust that has choked the unit are also signs the end is near. When that is the case we move straight to water heater replacement and size the new unit with you.

Tankless heaters have their own faults, usually error codes and mineral buildup in the heat exchanger. Those are covered under tankless water heater service.
