Signs the main sewer line is blocked.

One slow sink is a branch drain problem. A main line problem shows up everywhere at once. The lowest drain in the house usually complains first, which is why basement floor drains and shower pans are the early warning.

Watch for the pattern rather than a single symptom. Several of these together point to the line between the house and the street.

Gurgling toilets.

A toilet that bubbles when the washer drains is pushing air through a line that cannot move water.

Backup in the lowest fixture.

Sewage or gray water rising in a basement floor drain or tub while upstairs fixtures still drain slowly.

Sewer gas smell.

A sewage odor near drains or in the basement can mean standing waste in the line, not only a dry trap.

Multiple fixtures slow.

Toilets, tubs and the laundry standpipe all draining sluggishly on the same day.

Before we arrive: what to do while sewage is backing up.

These steps limit the mess and keep the line accessible. They are safe for any homeowner.

Stop all water use.

Do not flush, run the dishwasher or start a laundry load. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go but the lowest drain.

Find the cleanout.

Look for a capped pipe, usually near the foundation wall in the basement or just outside. Clear a path to it so the cable can go straight in.

Protect people and pets.

Keep children and animals away from standing sewage. Wear gloves and boots if you must walk through it, and never mix cleaning chemicals.

Document and move belongings.

Photograph the backup for your records, then lift boxes and electronics off the floor. Leave the cleanup until the line is open.

What Ridgewood's own sewer rules mean for your line.

The Village of Ridgewood runs its own Water Pollution Control Facility, so the public sewer your lateral connects to is a local system, not a distant utility. Your responsibility starts at the house and runs to the connection.

Village code, Chapter 238, says house sewers must be cast iron or vitrified clay beyond three feet from the building. Both materials are durable, and both have joints. Joints are where roots find their way in, which is why an older Ridgewood lateral can clog repeatedly in the same spot.

The code also makes connections to the sewer a job for a licensed plumber, and New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed master plumber. If a cable hits a joint offset, a lined section or a break, that is the moment to stop cabling and look with a camera.

Cabling versus a camera and a repair.

A cabling visit usually fits when.

  • The backup is sudden and the line drained fine last month.
  • Grease, paper or a wad of wipes is the likely cause.
  • The cleanout is accessible and the cable runs freely.

A camera look usually fits when.

  • The same drain backs up every few months.
  • The cable stops short or comes back with roots or clay.
  • You are buying, selling or about to dig near the line.

If only one fixture is slow, a smaller job like drain cleaning may be all that is needed. The point of the first call is to sort which one you have. For neighbors in Wyckoff, the same approach applies, and the Wyckoff service page lists the nearby coverage.
