Which drain is the problem.

Where the water backs up tells you how serious the clog is. One fixture is usually a local blockage. Several fixtures at once point at the main line.

One sink or tub.

Hair, soap film and kitchen grease collect in the trap and the first stretch of branch pipe. These clear with a hand auger or small cable.

Toilet and tub gurgling together.

Air is being pushed back through the traps because the line downstream is partly blocked. This is the early stage of a main line problem.

Basement floor drain.

A floor drain that burbles or overflows when the washer runs is connected to the main line. The clog is usually past the branch.

Laundry standpipe.

Lint and detergent residue build up fast. Water rising in the standpipe during the spin cycle is the warning.

Signs the main line is clogged.

  • Two or more fixtures drain slowly at the same time.
  • Water or sewage shows up in the lowest drain in the house when you flush or run a load.
  • A sewer gas smell near a floor drain or cleanout.
  • Bubbling in a toilet when the washer discharges.
  • A cleanout cap that is wet or stained.

Any of these is worth a call the same day. A main line that is only slow today can stop completely when the next shower or load of laundry runs.

Cable, jet or camera first.

The right tool depends on what is in the pipe. We choose after hearing the symptoms and looking at the access.

Emergency: when a drain backs up tonight, do this first.

What you do in the first few minutes decides how much cleanup follows.

Stop the water.

Do not flush, run the washer or use the dishwasher. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere, and the lowest opening is usually your basement.

Find the cleanout.

Look for a capped pipe near the main stack in the basement or at the outside foundation. Knowing where it is saves time when we arrive. Do not open it if sewage is rising.

Protect the floor.

Lift boxes and electronics off the floor and keep children and pets out of wet areas. Sewage water is not safe to touch bare handed.

Skip the chemicals.

Liquid drain openers sit in a standing clog and can burn skin and pipe. Tell us if you already used one so we can work safely.

Why Ridgewood lines clog and come back.

The local library's history pages show Ridgewood neighborhoods built between the 1890s and about 1950, so many sewer laterals are decades old. Village code calls for cast iron or vitrified clay house sewers beyond three feet from a building. Both materials can develop rough joints and cracks where roots and debris catch.

If you keep getting the same clog, the answer is rarely a stronger cable. It is a camera look to see whether the pipe has a belly, a root intrusion or a broken joint, which leads into sewer line repair.

There is also a village rule that matters in a backup. According to Chapter 238 of Ridgewood's code, a groundwater sump may not be connected to a discharge point tied to the sanitary sewer. A pump tied into the sewer can overload the line in heavy rain, so tell us if your sump discharges anywhere near a floor drain.
