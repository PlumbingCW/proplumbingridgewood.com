When a faucet needs a plumber and not a new washer.

Most faucets fail in one of four places, the cartridge, the seats and springs, the O-rings or the supply connections. Each one has its own symptom, and each repair is a different job.

The list below sorts what you see into what is usually wrong.

Common faucet symptoms and likely causes.
What you see.Likely cause.Usual fix.
Steady drip from the spout.Worn cartridge or seat.Replace cartridge or seats.
Water leaking at the handle.Worn O-ring or loose packing.Replace seals, tighten the nut.
Puddle under the sink.Cracked supply line or loose stop valve.Replace the line or valve.

Handle hard to turn.Mineral buildup in the cartridge.Clean, or replace the cartridge.

Water spraying from a faucet? Do this before we arrive.

Every fixture has its own shutoff. Finding it is the quickest fix.

Look under the sink.

Two small valves sit on the supply lines, hot on the left and cold on the right. Turn each clockwise until it stops.

If there is no valve.

Tubs and showers often have no local stop. Close the main water valve where the line enters the house.

Test the shutoff.

Open the faucet after closing the valve. If water still runs, the stop valve itself has failed and the main has to stay closed.

Dry and protect.

Mop the cabinet floor, set a bucket under the leak and open the doors so the wood can dry. Do not run the disposal if water is near it.

How a faucet repair is done.

  1. 1.

    Isolate the fixture.

    Local valves are closed and the faucet is drained. If the stops are old, we test them before relying on them.

    .
  2. 2.

    Take it apart.

    Handles, escutcheons and the cartridge come out, and we identify the make so we match the parts.

    .
  3. 3.

    Replace the worn parts.

    Cartridges, seats, springs and O-rings are replaced, and corroded stops or supply lines are replaced at the same time.

    .
  4. 4.

    Test hot, cold and under pressure.

    Both handles are cycled, the aerator is flushed and every joint is checked dry with a paper towel.

    .

If the faucet is old and the finish is worn through, parts for it may be hard to find and a new faucet is the better buy. Choose a fixture with a solid brass body and a ceramic cartridge. Those hold up longer than thin zinc bodies.

Hard water and your faucets in Ridgewood.

Ridgewood Water's published quality report lists hardness between 136 and 470 parts per million as calcium carbonate. That works out to roughly 8 to 27 grains per gallon, which ranges from hard to very hard.

Hard water leaves limescale on aerators, cartridges and spray heads. A faucet that sputters or has a weak stream is often clogged with scale, not broken. Cartridges that drag under mineral buildup wear faster.

The report we are reading was published in 2020 using 2019 data, so check the current report from Ridgewood Water for the latest numbers. What does not change is the habit of unscrewing the aerator every few months and soaking it in vinegar, which prevents many service calls.

Faucet trouble that is really another problem.

If water pressure is low at every faucet in the house, the faucet is not at fault. That points toward the water service, so call about service line repair instead of buying a new fixture.

A faucet that spits air and water together usually means the line was recently drained or opened upstream, and it should settle after a minute of running. A leaking faucet connected to a sink that also drains slowly may need drain cleaning, and a wet cabinet under a kitchen sink sometimes involves the disposal and not the faucet at all.
